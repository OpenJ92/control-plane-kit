This setup-only mixin composes real operation-session, desired-graph, planning, approval-request and saved-admission services. It inherits draft selection/catalogue fixtures rather than replacing their stores or planner. The ancestor trusts the Operations-suite database URL, creates a unique schema, uses schema-local connections/UoWs and drops the schema on cleanup. The product and DockerRuntime graph declarations are fixture data, not image pulls or provider execution. The existing Docker-backed suite supplies the permitted execution context.

program constructs a fresh DeploymentProgram each time. Optional stop modes replace the planner with StopBefore after admission, the approval service with StopBefore after planning, or approval with StopAfter after its real execute call. Thus InterruptedPreparation can occur between durable preparation stages. These are same-process exception seams, not physical process/database crashes or kill injection; they do not themselves prove recovery from arbitrary instruction boundaries. Ordinary services still own writes and transactions.

selected creates then selects a draft through catalogue commands. prepare_command reads workspace lineage and constructs a saved-revision input with a trusted fixture principal, expected current/desired projections and generation, stable title/comment and caller key. Clocks default to NOW and IDs to UUIDs; tests can override UoW, operations, clock or IDs. A fixture principal is not transport authentication. prepared_session follows the returned plan through actual stores to its owning session.

all_truth extends inherited catalogue/runtime snapshots with session and product rows. frozen_truth removes the session/action/plan/approval-request tables expected to change during preparation. These helpers are selected-table snapshots, not all durable state: notably saved-source association tests add cpk_saved_preparation_sources separately. An equality assertion on them does not establish complete database or provider immutability.

SAVED_ONLY_KEYS and expected_saved_metadata are independent protocol witnesses rather than calls to the production metadata helper. The latter derives a canonical JSON/SHA-256 commitment from the same specified public intent fields and renders the fixed metadata shape. It tests semantic agreement, not cryptographic authenticity, universal redaction or absence of every possible correlated bug. Keep it a small witness; do not turn it into a second admission implementation.
